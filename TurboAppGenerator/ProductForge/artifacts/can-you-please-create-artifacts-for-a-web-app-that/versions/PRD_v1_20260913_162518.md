# Product Requirements Document
## OEM–Dealer Data & Insights Platform (Working Title)

**Document Owner:** Product Management
**Status:** Draft for Stakeholder Review
**Version:** 1.0

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This document defines the requirements for a web-based application that gives Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) and their affiliated Dealers a shared platform to view, manage, and analyze operational data. The platform is built around three core capabilities explicitly requested in the product brief:

1. **Data Grids** — structured, interactive tabular views of operational data.
2. **Visualization/Analysis** — charts and analytical views built on the same underlying data.
3. **Conversational Chatbot** — a chat interface that allows users to query and interact with their data using natural language, instead of exclusively navigating grids and dashboards manually.

The platform must serve two distinct but related audiences — OEM staff (who need cross-dealer, aggregated visibility) and Dealer staff (who need focused visibility into their own dealership's data) — under a single, secured application with role- and tenant-aware access.

**[ASSUMPTION]** Because the original product idea did not specify the underlying business domain (e.g., vehicle inventory, sales, service/warranty claims, parts orders), this PRD treats the platform as a general **OEM–Dealer operational data management platform**, where the specific data domain(s) covered will be confirmed with stakeholders before feature-level data modeling begins (see Open Questions). All requirements below are written to be domain-agnostic where possible, and domain-specific examples are explicitly marked as illustrative, not committed scope.

## 2. Problem Statement

OEMs and their dealer networks operate on a common set of data (illustratively: inventory, orders, sales, claims, performance), but today:

- **OEMs** lack a consolidated, real-time view across all dealers, forcing manual data collection, spreadsheet consolidation, and delayed decision-making.
- **Dealers** lack self-service tools to inspect and analyze their own operational data without going through OEM-provided reports or manual requests, which are often slow and inflexible.
- **Both audiences** need to interpret data quickly, but data grids and dashboards alone require users to already know what question to ask and where to look — there is no fast, conversational way to ask "what changed" or "why" without a trained analyst.
- Data currently used by both parties is fragmented across mismatched formats, tools, and access levels, creating trust issues, duplicated effort, and slow time-to-insight.

**Core problem to solve:** OEMs and dealers need one shared, secured platform where each party can view the data relevant to their role, explore it visually, and get fast answers — without waiting on manual reporting cycles or misusing data outside their permission scope.

## 3. User Personas

**[ASSUMPTION]** Names, titles, and narrative detail below are illustrative personas constructed from the roles identified during discovery (OEM Admin/Analyst, Dealer Principal, Dealer Staff). Exact org titles should be validated with real customers.

### Persona 1: Priya Sharma — OEM Operations Analyst ("OEM Admin/Analyst" role)
- **Role:** Works at OEM headquarters, responsible for monitoring performance and data quality across all dealers in her region.
- **Goals:** Get a consolidated, accurate, real-time view of all dealer data; spot outliers/underperforming dealers quickly; answer ad-hoc leadership questions without building a new report each time.
- **Pain Points:** Currently relies on dealers to submit spreadsheets on a lag; no single source of truth; cross-dealer comparisons take days to compile.
- **Success looks like:** She can open the platform, see all dealers' data in one grid/dashboard, and ask the chatbot a direct question ("Which dealers had the largest change in X last month?") and get an immediate, trustworthy answer.

### Persona 2: Marcus Webb — Dealer Principal
- **Role:** Owns/runs a single dealership; accountable for his dealership's overall performance to the OEM.
- **Goals:** Monitor his dealership's data in real time; understand trends without needing a dedicated analyst; ensure his team's data is accurate before OEM reviews it.
- **Pain Points:** Only sees data the OEM chooses to send him, on the OEM's schedule; no ability to self-serve analysis; no fast way to ask questions about his own numbers.
- **Success looks like:** He logs in, sees only his dealership's data (never another dealer's), can slice/filter it himself, and can ask the chatbot plain-language questions about his own performance.

### Persona 3: Sam Ortiz — Dealer Staff (Sales/Service Coordinator)
- **Role:** Front-line staff member responsible for entering/maintaining day-to-day records at the dealership (illustrative: sales records, service entries).
- **Goals:** Quickly find and update specific records; avoid data-entry errors; get quick answers to routine questions without escalating to a manager.
- **Pain Points:** Limited system access today means constant back-and-forth with the Dealer Principal or OEM for basic lookups; no visibility into whether entered data is correct/complete.
- **Success looks like:** He can access a restricted, role-appropriate view of dealership data, edit only the fields he's permitted to, and ask the chatbot simple lookup questions instead of interrupting his manager.

## 4. Goals & Success Metrics

### 4.1 Business Goals
1. Give OEMs a single consolidated view of dealer data, replacing manual/periodic data consolidation.
2. Give dealers self-service access to their own data without waiting on OEM-provided reports.
3. Reduce time-to-insight for both OEM and dealer users by supplementing grids/dashboards with a conversational query interface.
4. Ensure strict, verifiable data access boundaries between OEM and dealer roles, and between dealers themselves.

| Metric | Baseline (Today) | Target (Post-Launch) | Measurement Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time for OEM analyst to produce a cross-dealer comparison view | Manual, days (assumed) | ≤ 5 minutes via grid/dashboard | Time-to-task testing with target users |
| Time for dealer user to answer a basic data question (e.g., "how many records changed this week") | Manual/ask someone (assumed) | ≤ 2 minutes using chatbot or grid | Time-to-task testing |

## 5. Feature Requirements

Features are prioritized using MoSCoW-style tiers (P0 = Must Have / launch blocker, P1 = Should Have, P2 = Nice to Have). **P0 is capped at 5 features by design** — anything not on this list is not required to ship v1.

### 5.1 P0 — Must Have (Launch Blockers)

#### P0-1: Role-Based, Tenant-Isolated Access Control
**Description:** Every user is assigned a role (illustratively: OEM Admin, OEM Analyst, Dealer Principal, Dealer Staff — **[ASSUMPTION]**, pending stakeholder confirmation of final role list) that determines what data (which dealer(s), which fields) they can view or edit. OEM roles may see data across dealers; dealer roles see only their own dealership's data.
**Why P0:** Without this, the platform cannot be safely used by two different organizations sharing one system — this is a data governance and trust prerequisite for every other feature.
**Acceptance Criteria:**
- A Dealer-role user can never view, export, or query (via grid, chart, or chatbot) another dealer's records, under any circumstance, including via chatbot free-text queries.
- An OEM-role user's visible data scope (single dealer vs. all dealers) matches their assigned permission level exactly.
- Every grid, chart, and chatbot response independently enforces the same access rules (no feature bypasses the access control).
- Attempting to access out-of-scope data returns a clear "not authorized" response rather than an error that leaks data existence.
- Access rules are auditable: every data access event is logged with user, role, and scope for compliance review.

#### P0-2: Interactive Data Grid
**Description:** A tabular view of operational records that users can view, sort, filter, and search according to their permission scope. Authorized users can edit records inline, subject to field-level permissions.
**Why P0:** Explicitly requested as a core capability; it is the primary way users will inspect and manage raw data.
**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Users can sort and filter the grid by any visible column.
- Users can search across visible records.
- Editable fields are only editable for users with the appropriate role/permission; read-only fields are visually distinguished and cannot be modified regardless of client-side manipulation.
- Every edit is validated against field rules (required/optional, type, format, uniqueness where applicable) before being saved, and invalid edits are rejected with a clear, specific error message (not a generic failure).
- Users can export the currently filtered/visible view of the grid, restricted to their permitted data scope.
- Grid performance remains usable (no unresponsive UI) when loading the maximum expected record volume for a single dealer view and for a full OEM cross-dealer view. **[ASSUMPTION: exact volume thresholds to be defined once data domain and scale are confirmed — see Open Questions.]**

#### P0-3: Visualization & Analysis Dashboards
**Description:** Chart-based visual representations of the same underlying data available in the grid, enabling trend and comparison analysis without manual chart-building.
**Why P0:** Explicitly requested as a core capability ("visualization (analysis)"); grids alone are insufficient for pattern recognition and executive-level review.
**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Users can view at least trend-over-time and comparison-across-entities visualizations relevant to their role's data scope (e.g., a dealer sees trends for their own dealership; an OEM user can compare across dealers).
- Visualizations respect the same access-control rules as the grid — a dealer-scoped user never sees another dealer's data represented in a chart, even in aggregate/anonymized form, unless explicitly designed and approved as an aggregate benchmark (see Open Questions).
- Every visualization can be filtered by the same dimensions available in the grid (e.g., date range, category), so grid and dashboard views stay consistent with each other.
- Underlying data for any chart can be traced back to (or cross-referenced with) the corresponding grid records, so users can move from a visual insight to the underlying detail.

#### P0-4: Conversational Chatbot for Data Q&A
**Description:** A chat interface where users can ask natural-language questions about the data they are authorized to see, and receive relevant answers, summaries, or references to the corresponding grid/chart data.
**Why P0:** Explicitly requested as a core capability; it is a primary differentiator versus "just another dashboard tool."
**Acceptance Criteria:**
- Chatbot only answers using data within the requesting user's permitted access scope — it must apply the same role/tenant restrictions as the grid and dashboards (no exceptions via clever phrasing or indirect queries).
- Chatbot correctly answers a defined set of representative business questions (to be finalized as a test suite) with accuracy validated against the underlying data.
- When the chatbot cannot answer confidently or the question is out of its supported scope, it clearly states this rather than guessing or fabricating an answer.
- Every chatbot answer that references specific data points can be traced back to (or linked to) the corresponding grid/dashboard record for verification.
- Chatbot interactions are logged for quality review and access-control auditing, consistent with P0-1.

#### P0-5: Authentication & Account/Role Provisioning
**Description:** Users must securely log in, and each account must be provisioned with an organization (OEM or specific dealer) and a role before they can access any data.
**Why P0:** This is the foundational gate that makes P0-1 through P0-4 enforceable; without controlled account provisioning, tenant/role isolation cannot exist in practice.
**Acceptance Criteria:**
- A user cannot access any grid, dashboard, or chatbot feature without successfully authenticating.
- Every account is associated with exactly one organizational scope (a specific dealer, or the OEM) and exactly one role at a time.
- An administrator (OEM Admin) can provision, modify, and deactivate user accounts and their role/scope assignments.
- Deactivated accounts immediately lose all access, including any in-progress sessions. **[ASSUMPTION: "immediately" to be defined precisely — real-time vs. session-expiry-based — pending technical scoping in TRD.]**

### 5.2 P1 — Should Have

#### P1-1: Saved Views & Filters
Users can save frequently used grid filters/sorts and dashboard configurations for quick re-access.
**Acceptance Criteria:** A saved view persists across sessions for the user who created it; saved views respect the same access-control rules as live data (a saved view cannot be used to bypass permission changes made after saving).

#### P1-2: Bulk Edit / Bulk Actions on Grid
Authorized users can select multiple rows and apply an edit or action to all of them at once.
**Acceptance Criteria:** Bulk actions are only available to roles explicitly permitted for bulk operations; every record affected by a bulk edit is individually validated using the same rules as a single edit (P0-2), and any records that fail validation are reported individually rather than silently skipped.

#### P1-3: Field-Level Data Validation Rules (Extended)
Beyond basic required/type/format validation in P0-2, support defined business rules such as uniqueness constraints and cross-field consistency checks. **[ASSUMPTION: specific rules depend on final data domain — flagged for confirmation.]**
**Acceptance Criteria:** Each defined validation rule is documented, testable, and consistently enforced across grid edit, bulk edit, and any chatbot-initiated data changes (if chatbot write actions are in scope — see Open Questions).

#### P1-4: Data Export Scheduling / Recurring Reports
Users can schedule recurring exports of a saved grid view or dashboard for delivery outside the platform.
**Acceptance Criteria:** Scheduled exports only ever include data within the requesting user's permitted access scope, matching P0-1 rules exactly.

#### P1-5: Chatbot-to-Grid/Dashboard Drill-Down
When the chatbot answers a question, the user can click through directly from the chat answer to the filtered grid view or chart that produced that answer.
**Acceptance Criteria:** Clicking through from a chatbot answer opens the corresponding grid/dashboard pre-filtered to match the chatbot's answer, and displays only records within the user's permitted scope.

#### P1-6: Multi-Region / Multi-Currency / Localization Support
**[ASSUMPTION — raised by Business Analyst, not yet confirmed by stakeholders]** If OEM operations span multiple countries, grids, dashboards, and chatbot responses need to correctly localize currency, units of measure, and language.
**Acceptance Criteria:** To be defined once regional scope is confirmed (see Open Questions). Placeholder requirement: no numeric or date value is displayed in an ambiguous or incorrect regional format for the viewing user's assigned locale.

#### P1-7: Notification / Alerting on Data Changes
Users receive an in-app notification when a data condition they care about changes (e.g., a record they follow is updated).
**Acceptance Criteria:** Notifications respect access-control scope; a user is never notified about a change to data outside their permitted scope.

### 5.3 P2 — Nice to Have

#### P2-1: Chatbot-Initiated Chart Generation
Users can ask the chatbot to generate a new, ad-hoc visualization directly from a natural-language request, rather than only querying existing dashboards.
**Acceptance Criteria:** Generated charts are subject to the same access-control and data-accuracy validation as P0-3 and P0-4.

#### P2-2: Proactive Chatbot Insights
The chatbot proactively surfaces notable patterns or anomalies to users (e.g., "Your numbers changed significantly this week") rather than only responding to direct questions.
**Acceptance Criteria:** Proactive insights only surface data within the user's permitted scope and are clearly distinguishable from user-initiated Q&A.

#### P2-3: Mobile-Responsive Layout
The web application is usable on tablet/mobile screen sizes for on-the-go review (not a dedicated native app).
**Acceptance Criteria:** Core grid, dashboard, and chatbot functions remain usable (readable, operable without horizontal scrolling breaking layout) on common tablet screen widths.

#### P2-4: User-Level Customization of Dashboard Layout
Users can rearrange or choose which visualizations appear on their personal dashboard.
**Acceptance Criteria:** Layout customization is per-user, persists across sessions, and does not affect other users' views.

## 6. Out of Scope (Explicitly Excluded from This PRD)

To prevent scope creep, the following are **not** part of this product's requirements unless a future PRD revision states otherwise:

- **Native mobile applications** (iOS/Android) — only a responsive web experience is in scope, and only as P2.
- **Offline mode / offline data access** — the platform assumes an active connection.
- **Financial transaction processing** (payments, invoicing, billing) — the platform is for data viewing/analysis/management, not transaction execution, unless later explicitly scoped.
- **Direct integrations with specific external systems** (e.g., ERP, DMS, telematics, third-party marketplaces) — no such integration was specified in the product brief; any future integration requires its own requirements definition.
- **Automated write-actions performed by the chatbot on underlying data** (e.g., chatbot directly editing/deleting records) are out of scope for P0–P1; the chatbot is a **read/query and navigation** interface unless a future revision explicitly adds write capability (see Open Questions).
- **Support for languages beyond the primary operating language of the OEM** — full multi-language support is not committed; only P1 localization of numbers/currency/units is flagged, and even that is pending confirmation.
- **Data science / predictive modeling / forecasting capabilities** — visualization and chatbot Q&A are about describing existing/historical data, not predicting future outcomes, unless explicitly added later.
- **Public or unauthenticated access to any part of the platform** — every feature requires authenticated, role-scoped access (per P0-5); there is no public-facing version of this product.

## 7. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Domain ambiguity**: the product brief did not specify the exact business data domain (inventory, sales, claims, etc.), risking misaligned feature design. | High — could require rework of grid/dashboard content | High | Resolve via stakeholder workshop before feature-level data definitions are finalized; tracked as Open Question #1. |
| **Cross-tenant data leakage**: a bug or oversight in grid, dashboard, or chatbot logic exposes one dealer's data to another dealer or unauthorized OEM role. | Critical — breach of trust, potential legal/contractual exposure | Medium | Access control (P0-1) is treated as a cross-cutting requirement validated independently in grid, dashboard, and chatbot; dedicated access-control test suite and audit logging required before launch. |
| **Chatbot inaccuracy or fabricated answers ("hallucination")**: chatbot gives a plausible-sounding but incorrect answer about business data. | High — erodes trust in the entire platform, could drive incorrect business decisions | Medium-High | Chatbot must state uncertainty rather than fabricate (P0-4 acceptance criteria); all data-referencing answers must be traceable to source records; ongoing QA test suite required before and after launch. |
| **Low adoption by dealer staff** due to unfamiliarity or perceived complexity, undermining self-service goals. | Medium — fails business goal #2 | Medium | Define clear onboarding flow requirements (see Open Question #6) and track weekly active usage against the 70% target in Section 4.2; iterate based on usage analytics. |
| **Data quality issues at input** (bad/missing data entered by dealer staff) undermine grid, dashboard, and chatbot trustworthiness regardless of platform quality. | Medium-High | High | Field-level validation (P0-2, P1-3) enforced at point of entry; data trust metric tracked explicitly in Section 4.2. |
| **Scope creep** from stakeholders wanting to add domain-specific features (e.g., specific report types) before core platform (grids/viz/chatbot + access control) is stable. | Medium | Medium | Strict P0 cap (5 features) enforced in this PRD; all domain-specific asks routed through Open Questions/future PRD revisions rather than added ad hoc. |
| **Regulatory/compliance exposure** if OEM/dealer data includes personally identifiable or otherwise regulated information and access controls are insufficient. | High (if applicable) | Unknown — domain not yet confirmed | Flagged as Open Question #7; compliance requirements must be gathered once data domain is confirmed, before final sign-off. |

## 8. Open Questions (Must Be Resolved Before/During Detailed Design)

1. **Data domain**: What specific business data will this platform manage and analyze (e.g., vehicle inventory, sales performance, warranty/service claims, parts orders)? This determines all grid columns, chart types, and chatbot question scope.
2. **Multi-region scope**: Does the OEM operate across multiple countries/currencies/languages? If yes, P1-6 (localization) requirements need to be fully specified rather than left as a placeholder.
3. **Final role list and permissions matrix**: Is the four-role model (OEM Admin, OEM Analyst, Dealer Principal, Dealer Staff) accurate and complete, or are there additional roles (e.g., regional OEM manager, dealer service manager)?
4. **Chatbot write capability**: Should the chatbot ever be allowed to initiate data changes (e.g., "mark this record as resolved"), or should it remain strictly read/query as currently scoped in Out of Scope?
5. **Aggregate/benchmark visibility for dealers**: Should dealer-role users ever see anonymized, aggregated cross-dealer benchmarks (e.g., "your performance vs. regional average") without seeing individual competitor dealer data? This affects P0-3 acceptance criteria.
6. **Onboarding requirements**: What is the expected onboarding flow for a newly provisioned user (OEM-invited dealer staff, etc.)? Not yet defined in this PRD and needed to support the adoption success metric.
7. **Compliance/regulatory requirements**: Does the confirmed data domain involve personally identifiable information or industry-specific regulatory requirements (e.g., data residency, retention rules) that must shape access control and audit logging requirements?
8. **Expected data volume and scale per tenant and cross-tenant**: Needed to properly define grid and dashboard performance acceptance criteria (currently flagged as assumption in P0-2).
9. **Historical data depth**: How far back should visualizations and chatbot queries be able to analyze (e.g., trailing 12 months, all history)?
